Prepared 2026-11-11, bounded source research; no experiment authorized or launched. Parent Research owns selection of one primary experiment and one contingent alternative.

## Decision-useful conclusion

**Training fit, new-policy transfer within a trained mechanism, unseen combinations of known operations, and unseen algorithms are four different claims.** Original SCAN and CFQ experiments show that near-perfect training/random-split performance can coexist with very poor compositional transfer. Conversely, one tiny diagnostic suite cannot establish a general incapacity. D3 intentionally measures the second claim, not the third or fourth. Do not label a D3 success “unseen rule learning,” or a D3 failure “failure on unseen mechanisms.”

There is credible positive evidence for **training-set compositional augmentation** and **explicit intermediate-state supervision**, but neither is a plug-in guarantee for Jeff. GECA is closer to Jeff's current training interface; scratchpad generation is a substantially different computation mechanism from a one-pass candidate scorer. Curriculum evidence favors retaining hard examples rather than assuming easy-to-hard ordering is beneficial. In particular, low-data scratchpads and naive curricula have published negative results.

## Training-only augmentation design input (not selected, not generated)

If the parent chooses a data intervention, the closest-fit evidence supports **small, verified, structure-aware variation across policies**, not more near-duplicate prose or fitting D3 mistakes. This is a Jeff-specific hypothesis inspired by S3/S5, not an empirical result from those papers.

- Select at most four mechanisms using **training metadata and train-only fit diagnostics**, independently of D3 cases/errors. Example-selection rules must be frozen before seeing any protected diagnostic rows. Do not request D3 examples or generate paraphrases of validation/probes/final. Existing public D3 aggregate success/failure may choose the intervention class, not the cases to imitate.
- Proposed budget: **64 policy roots**,16 per selected mechanism; preassign48 to fitting and16 to a training-internal transfer monitor, then create4 bounded cases per root:192 fit and64 monitor decisions. This internal monitor is not official held-out validation or an unseen-mechanism benchmark, and must never later be folded into fitting after using its outcomes. All belong to a separately versioned training-development collection if authorized. If the owner requires absolutely no new monitoring split, use only192 fitting rows and existing frozen development reporting; do not silently relabel monitored data as training.
- Author root specifications independently from a training-side mechanism definition, with explicit policy interpretation, varied thresholds/operator choices, and an executable solver. Cluster roots sharing a formal policy/derivation skeleton if “independent” roots actually reuse it. Count unique formal policies, mechanisms, cases, and teacher sources separately. Source-policy disjointness precedes rendering; all policy-counterfactuals, paraphrases and variants inherit their root split.
- Within a fitting root, vary facts/active branches, relevant versus irrelevant material, and bounded operation depth. Include contrastive target-changing perturbations **only if the solver independently recomputes the target**. Counterbalance target/choice length; preserve candidate semantics rather than padding correct answers. Do not substitute fragments and retain labels blindly (GECA's ill-typed outputs are directly relevant).
- Keep a mixed population of short and harder in-range cases in every phase, rather than graduating entirely away from easy cases or never seeing hard ones. Hold total presentations/updates constant against a matched baseline if the parent wants a data-content causal comparison; adding192 rows at fixed epochs otherwise changes both coverage and compute. Do not confound a new curriculum, rationale objective, backbone, and sampling change in one run.
- Track source-policy macro accuracy/loss and target-category counts alongside row metrics. Favor more policy roots over multiplying paraphrases of one policy. Fit first, new-policy transfer second; do not promise extrapolation to unseen algorithms.

### Estimated costs, explicitly not measurements

Assuming existing Data solvers/renderers can be reused and no service calls: adapting4 mechanism producers and specifying policy controls **2–4 engineer-hours**; split/provenance/structural checks **0.5–1h**; independent verifier/review **1–3h** (solver disagreement handling can exceed this). These are judgment estimates, not local time observations. A minimal independent solver needs separate reasoning about rule semantics, not importing the authoring solver twice. A human label-hidden check of2 cases/root at2–4minutes each costs **128 reviews /4.3–8.5 reviewer-hours**; reviewing only16 stratified roots ×2 cases costs **32 reviews /1.1–2.1h** and is weaker coverage. Executable verification can cover all256 decisions with bounded CPU work, but no measured runtime is available; trace complexity is O(rows × computation steps), and solver correctness remains a risk. No new labels/rows or verifier code were made here.

At3 epochs and group batch4,192 extra fitting rows imply576 extra presentations and144 nominal updates if simply appended (12.8% extra rows relative to1,500). Candidate forward/backward cost also depends on choices and token lengths; wall-clock/memory cannot be inferred from these counts. Holding the total update budget instead avoids that compute increase but reduces inherited-data exposure. A fixed-length22M encoder retains approximately the same model-state memory, but batch activation/token-length cost can rise; **24GiB fit is not measured here**. Model owns scheduling. No paid teacher call is included;£0 actual/pending.

Scratchpad alternative is **higher cost and interface risk**, not this strand's default data edit: requires a generative model/objective or an explicit iterative subdecision interface and inference-time state generation. Merely creating auxiliary intermediate-choice training questions can reuse Jeff's scorer, but does not grant extra computation at deployment. For4 mechanisms, assume another **2–6h** to define/check state schemas and intermediate validators; a4–8-state trace over192 fitting decisions yields768–1,536 extra state targets, with model work roughly scaling with those extra presentations before token effects. Free-text rationales need factual/stepwise validators, not fluency scoring. A teacher-generated explanation agreeing with its own answer is not independent verification. This work is likely too large to bundle with a clean next comparison under the2026-11-12 23:59 London deadline unless infrastructure already exists; no local availability was assumed.

## D3-conditioned action table for parent synthesis

| Observed public aggregates, once available | Interpretation / bounded next-action input |
|---|---|
| Poor train fit AND poor D3 | Prioritize train-owned label/input/answer-bias diagnostics and fitability; do not announce a compositional-transfer cure. More independently varied policies can make fit harder. Scratchpad evidence does not rescue an unverified fit failure. |
| Strong train fit, weak D3 | Strongest case for independently sourced/verified new fitting policies and semantic counterfactual coverage, with frozen evaluation boundary. Not proof that this intervention works; test policy transfer rather than replaying known D3 policies. |
| Strong train fit AND good D3, but weak broader held-out named-mechanism results | The gap is beyond seen-mechanism/new-policy transfer, or is entangled with different distribution/construction. Do not spend all effort on more paraphrases of seen mechanisms; retain checkpoint and consider a distinct known-primitives/new-composition hypothesis. D3 cannot certify algorithm novelty. |
| Mixed D3 by mechanism or aggregate improvements with worse loss | Keep all mechanisms, compare policy macro rank and probability outcomes; avoid selecting a tiny recovered group as success (M5 already illustrates that risk). Sparse strata cannot justify case-targeted data. |
| Frozen Qwen0.5B comparison is better / worse | Either result may inform practicality only. Different capacity, pretraining, prompts, context and computation confound it. Do not infer scratchpad efficacy or architecture causality unless actual frozen prompt/method tests that contrast. No E5 outcome assumed here. |

No numerical success cutoff is invented after outcomes. Parent/Evaluation should prespecify a practically meaningful paired policy/mechanism criterion and probability-quality guardrail before a run; published benchmark gains are not Jeff acceptance thresholds.
