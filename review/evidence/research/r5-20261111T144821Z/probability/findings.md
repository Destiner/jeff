## 1. Ranking is not probability estimation

Let `s_i = f(context, question, option_i)` and `p_i = exp(s_i)/sum_j exp(s_j)`. Grouped-choice CE is `-log p_target`. Multiclass Brier is `sum_i (p_i - 1[i=target])^2` (sum convention, not divided by option count). Both losses are strictly proper for a categorical outcome distribution in expectation [S1, §3.1, examples 1 and 3]. Accuracy only asks whether the largest score wins; it cannot identify the whole distribution [S1, example 4].

Practical implications (mathematical deductions, not measured Jeff effects):

- A margin-ranking/hinge objective can reward the correct ordering without identifying a probability scale. Once a deterministic example clears its margin, many score configurations have zero loss but different softmax probabilities. Monotone score transforms preserve order while changing softmax. This does **not** mean every ranking method is incapable of probability estimation: a ranking-trained scorer may be calibrated separately; the ranking criterion alone is insufficient evidence.
- CE is already a relative, choice-group objective, not independent relevance regression. Retrieval relevance scores—even with a retained head—are not automatically categorical correctness probabilities after normalization.
- Better log loss without better top-1 is legitimate distribution improvement, not proof of better decisions; better top-1 with worse log loss is also possible. Guo et al. explicitly observe that disconnect [S2, §3/Fig.3]. Jeff's supplied M5 failed its fixed conjunction: neither a recovered group alone nor probability softening should silently replace that decision rule.
- Brier is bounded in [0,2] under the sum convention, while log loss diverges when target mass approaches zero. Reporting both reveals whether a few extreme mistakes dominate the mean; never remove such groups after seeing their influence. Brier is a proper alternative, but this literature does not establish that switching Jeff's training to Brier would improve rule learning or sample efficiency. No loss-switch run is justified by these sources alone.
- Variable choice counts change baselines: uniform predictions have log loss `log K` and Brier `1 - 1/K`. Compare models on exactly the same decisions; also report by K where denominators allow. Avoid rescaling Brier by each row's K and still presenting it as the same fixed proper scoring objective without explaining the changed weighting.

## 2. What Jeff's probabilities can mean

**Safe current wording:** “Normalized scores over the caller-supplied alternatives.” A stronger interpretation is an estimated conditional distribution over the selected target, *given this exact context, question, alternatives and labeling convention*. Calling it a probability of real-world correctness additionally needs trustworthy outcomes, representative assessment and evidence of reliability. Teacher-labeled validation establishes agreement with that teacher's selection, not independent truth.

Categorical proper-scoring results assume a finite set of mutually exclusive outcome events [S1, §3.1]. For Jeff:

- The contract chooses exactly one target, but choices can still overlap semantically or omit all acceptable answers in actual use. Training on single target IDs does not establish that callers supply exhaustive, disjoint real-world events.
- If no supplied choice is correct, softmax still sums to one and must allocate the entire mass. Neither a high maximum nor low entropy detects missing-answer coverage by construction. “None of the above” is a distinct task option only if explicitly supplied and semantically supported; it must not be invented or inferred from a low threshold.
- Ambiguity (multiple defensible answers), insufficient context, and model ignorance are different. A low score gap can arise from any of them, or merely small score scale. It does not identify which. A single hard label cannot identify a distribution over defensible interpretations. Do not manufacture soft targets from uniform label smoothing and call them measured ambiguity.
- Abstaining is an action outside choosing a supplied answer, not another outcome probability unless the contract explicitly defines one. Preserve the current prediction schema; a reporting-only rejection policy can be evaluated without silently adding abstention/none-of-above outputs.

**Choice-set dependence:** For independent candidate scores, reordering the same alternatives simply permutes p (apart from numerical effects and deterministic ties), assuming evaluation mode, identical pair rendering/truncation and no set-dependent formatting. But adding/removing a candidate changes every denominator. The ratio `p_i/p_j = exp(s_i-s_j)` stays fixed when a third option is added. This is a structural independence-of-irrelevant-alternatives property, not evidence of sensible uncertainty. Duplicating a meaning under a second ID can take mass away from the original while leaving its score unchanged. Probability must therefore be qualified by the exact supplied option set, not treated as a context-only confidence.

These are architectural deductions, not new empirical Jeff order/option interventions. Exact order invariance does not prove semantic robustness or stability to distractors. Existing order-pair aggregates can check implementation consistency without new inference.

## 3. Encoder pair-softmax versus decoder answer-symbol scores

For an independent encoder pair scorer, the opaque option IDs should not be input features. There is no A/B/C prior to inherit, but set normalization, wording and truncation still matter. If the scorer actually consumes all alternatives jointly, this order-invariance argument no longer applies; verify architecture rather than assuming it from the word “encoder.”

For a decoder prompted with all alternatives, the answer-symbol probability is initially a next-token language-model probability under the *whole ordered prompt*. Restricting to A/B/C and renormalizing computes `P(symbol_i | prompt, symbol in allowed set)`, for the precise tokenizer/prefix convention. It is not automatically `P(option_i is correct | task)`. It discards mass on all other continuations; if an allowed symbol is multiple tokens, next-token-only scoring is not its complete string likelihood. Complete option-text likelihood and length-normalized text likelihood are also different scoring rules, not interchangeable correctness probabilities.

Zheng et al. study 20 causal decoder models on MMLU, ARC-Challenge and CommonsenseQA with two to five choices [S3, §2.1]. They find substantial selection bias, primarily answer-ID token bias in their experiments, with less regular residual position bias [§2.4]. Counterevidence against an easy fix: removing IDs often reduces accuracy [§2.5], and position bias varies by model/task. Their open models are 7B–70B-class (plus GPT-3.5 API), **not 0.5B Qwen**; do not extrapolate an effect size or mandate PriDe for frozen P3. PriDe/permutation averaging would change the scoring recipe and cost; no post-outcome correction is proposed here.

For the existing frozen comparison, ask only for the already authorized public aggregate report: ID-remapped order flip rate, probability differences, invalid/missing output coverage and exact symbol scoring definition. Do not assume unseen order variants exist. Decoder-versus-encoder results remain confounded by capacity, pretraining, prompt and context budget, not a pure architecture experiment.

## 4. Teacher distributions are not verbal confidence

Hinton et al. transfer an actual vector of teacher class probabilities, softened at temperature T, and match it with student soft-target CE alongside hard-label CE [S5, §2]. Their `T^2` gradient scaling and shared transfer temperature concern **distillation training**, not post-hoc confidence calibration. Matching centered logits is a high-temperature special case [§2.1], not a license to regress arbitrary uncentered logits.

A teacher's generated “90% confident” is text from a separate elicitation procedure, not its option-logit distribution. Neither is a calibrated correctness guarantee merely because a strong teacher produced it. Hard teacher labels in Jeff are weak supervision/hard-target imitation; they do not contain the dark-knowledge distribution assumed by soft distillation. A decoder's allowed-symbol distribution is also not verbal confidence, but still needs the semantic qualifications above. No extra teacher call or fabricated probability target is warranted within this assignment.

Evidence gap: Hinton's speech example uses roughly 85M-parameter acoustic networks and ~700M frame examples; even its 3%-data experiment uses about 20M examples [S5, §§4,6]. MNIST's 60,000 examples and fixed digit labels also differ materially from 1,500 correlated, changing-choice language decisions. Published sample-efficiency results do not certify benefits for Jeff.

## 5. Safe tiny-development protocol (proposal only)

Evaluation owns any execution and protected inputs. This subagent read no raw Evaluation/probe/final files, ran no saved-prediction calculations, and obtained no new E4/E5 aggregates. The following uses only authorized non-final outputs in the owner's environment; Research should receive aggregates, not cases for Data authoring.

1. **Freeze first:** one existing checkpoint/scoring recipe, label basis, coverage denominator, source-group inventory, metric conventions, split assignment and comparison criterion. Declare all prior adaptive use of dev data. A new partition of repeatedly inspected validation is not retrospectively untouched.
2. **Keep evidence populations separate:** inherited60, new240, probe24/12 groups, D3 96/48 policies/8 intentionally seen mechanisms, and any frozen decoder population. Use actual released inventory rather than treating those contextual counts as independently checked. D3 is new-policy/seen-mechanism diagnostic evidence, not unseen-mechanism generalization. Never count reversals/paraphrases as new independent cases.
3. **Unfitted measurements first:** top-1/teacher agreement or independently verified correctness as appropriate; mean natural-log CE; sum-convention Brier; prediction failures; and row-weighted plus source-group-macro scores. Show all group contributions and family/K denominators. Confidence bins are a descriptive secondary display with counts, not an ECE leaderboard or promotion target. With few groups even five bins may be too fine.
4. **Only one fitted scalar:** if there are enough source groups to populate all families, preassign three source-disjoint folds, keeping the broadest known related source/template unit intact. Fit a global positive T to group-macro NLL on two folds, report held-out fold metrics on the third, and rotate. Use a prespecified bounded interval such as T in [0.1,10], log-spaced search plus a deterministic refinement, with T=1 included; report boundary hits and per-fold T instability. These bounds/folds are proposed engineering choices, not literature-derived optima. Do not try per-family, per-K, per-position, isotonic or matrix alternatives after seeing results. If groups are too few, report unfitted metrics only.
5. **Use probabilities if logits are absent:** for positive unrounded p, `q_i = p_i^(1/T) / sum_j p_j^(1/T)` is equivalent to scaling original logits. True zeros/rounded outputs lose information; disclose any epsilon convention and do not call reconstructed values original logits. This cannot recover decoder vocabulary mass discarded during normalization.
6. **Report paired differences, not a certificate:** cross-fitted group-macro NLL/Brier differences versus T=1; identical top-1 and within-example ranks are expected. Group-bootstrap descriptive intervals may be reported if enough source groups exist, resampling groups rather than rows; with only a handful of mechanisms/source constructions they are unstable and do not address distribution shift. Cross-fitting T reduces fit/evaluate reuse, but does not undo checkpoint selection on dev or repeated human tuning.
7. **Selective prediction:** show fixed, prespecified coverage points (e.g. 100%,75%,50%) and corresponding accepted error plus accepted row/group counts; tie policy fixed in advance. Choose the confidence statistic before examining outcomes. Treat these curves as diagnostics of error ranking, not guarantees. Keep empirical fixed-coverage summaries separate from a deployable fixed threshold, which would need calibration-partition selection and independent assessment. Never choose a threshold that happens to erase known failed groups.

A positive global T preserves each decision's class ordering, **not necessarily the ordering of maximum confidence across different multiclass decisions**. Therefore selective coverage sets can change after temperature scaling. Logit margin and confidence/entropy rankings are different; changing between them is another model-selection degree of freedom. Good rejection ranking need not require calibrated absolute probabilities [S4, §4]; calibration and selection are related but distinct goals.

For utility: if a correct accepted action costs 0, wrong action costs C, and abstention costs A, the ideal calibrated-probability rule accepts when `C*(1-p_correct) <= A`. More general asymmetric utilities require a full outcome/action utility table, not top-1 confidence alone. Unknown utility and uncalibrated p prevent claiming this is optimal in Jeff. Caller-chosen A/C must not be tuned for a flattering demo.

## 6. Costs, stop conditions and limits before the deadline

**Estimates/assumptions, not local measurements:** a saved-output implementation should take roughly 1–2 engineer-hours using existing scoring/storage interfaces; fitting one scalar on <=300 rows and <=6 alternatives is tiny CPU arithmetic (hundreds of thousands to low millions of scalar evaluations for a bounded search). Likely seconds to minutes including parsing/aggregate export on a laptop, negligible additional memory relative to the ML stack, zero additional model inference and £0 external spend. Actual code/data availability governs feasibility; no measured runtime is claimed. Group-bootstrap implementation/reporting adds time and is optional, not a prerequisite.

Temperature scaling cannot repair wrong answer ranks, missing reasoning, teacher errors, absent options, or transfer to new mechanisms. Guo's experiments assume matched train/validation/test distributions and use roughly 872–25,000 calibration examples across fixed-label tasks [S2, §§4–5]. Their Reuters counterexample shows calibration need not improve. Selective classification guarantees in S4 assume IID labeled sampling and use thousands to tens of thousands of calibration examples, unlike Jeff's correlated/adaptively reused tiny dev set.

Even an ideal fixed selector with zero errors among n IID accepted cases has a one-sided 95% binomial upper error bound `1 - 0.05^(1/n)` (deduction from S4's binomial-bound equation). At n=12 this is ~22.1%, not 0%; correlations/adaptation make a naive row-level application still less defensible. This is an illustrative bound, not a Jeff calculation or risk certificate.

**Cannot certify by hackathon from this evidence:** calibrated correctness on arbitrary caller choices, low-risk rejection on novel domains, robust none-of-above detection, an epistemic/ambiguity decomposition, unseen-mechanism transfer, or decoder/encoder architectural superiority. Cross-fitted NLL/Brier improvement is useful diagnostic evidence only. Any saved fitted T would be a new versioned artifact/recipe owned by Model/Runtime, requiring an explicit owner decision; no automatic promotion or new approval gate.
