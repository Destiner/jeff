## Decision-ready conclusion

**Do not replace Jeff's grouped-choice CE with generic instruction/rationale tuning on the strength of these papers.** Instruction tuning describes a training distribution/interface, not an inherently superior loss: answer-symbol instruction tuning can itself use a choice CE. Jeff already consumes natural-language instructions/questions/options. Its 22M pair scorer can learn discriminative compatibility without learning to emit language; retaining that objective is the lowest-cost anchor. Published rationale-distillation gains come from generative T5 students at least ~10× larger, predominantly a 540B teacher, and task-specific training—not a 22M scalar scorer learning general rule execution from 1,500 correlated decisions. [S1–S5]

**Soft-option distillation is a technically clean possible intervention, but only if a useful, authorized teacher distribution actually exists.** A teacher's selected answer or statement “90% confident” is not its option logit distribution. Qwen0.5B's frozen comparison is not yet evidence that it is a suitable teacher. Its ~22× parameter advantage over a 22M encoder does not guarantee better truth, rule transfer, or calibrated probabilities. Do not train on diagnostic/validation predictions. No train teacher probabilities were inspected or assumed available.

**The strongest contrary evidence is inside the attractive papers:** rationale+label concatenation sometimes hurts; small-model instruction transfer is recipe-dependent; symbol binding fails in some models; high-temperature matching can overburden a small student. None establishes a cheap universal cure for Jeff's narrow shortcuts or the failed M5 criterion. [S1 §§2.1,3; S2 §4.4; S3 §4; S4 §4.2; S5 §3.3]

## The objective distinction that matters

For candidate logits `s_i`, Jeff's `p_i = softmax_i(s)` and `L_hard = −log p_y` directly train relative choice selection. Independent candidate encoding means every score sees context/question/that candidate; candidates interact only through normalization. This is structurally permutation-equivariant when deterministic inference and mapping are correct. It can be restrictive when correctness inherently requires comparing candidates (“most specific”/“best of these”), but none of the reviewed sources establishes that this restriction caused Jeff's errors.

An instruction-tuned decoder can see all choices jointly and predict one answer symbol. At a verified single-token answer boundary it can also use `softmax` over allowed-symbol logits. Full-vocabulary symbol CE is different: `−log P_vocab(a_y) = −log P(a_y | allowed) − log P_vocab(allowed)`. Thus full-vocabulary CE additionally trains allowed-format probability mass; choice-normalized CE does not. Token-level language/rationale CE spends gradient on wording and format in addition to the decision. These are mathematical distinctions, not observed Jeff outcomes.

Instruction text is not itself a learning algorithm. Changing “Question:” into an elaborate instruction does not import the broad training mixture/pretraining of FLAN or Qwen. Grouped CE can coexist with instructions, hard labels, smoothing or KD.

## Hard labels, logits, verbal confidence, and truth

1. **Hard-label distillation:** use the teacher's selected option as a pseudo-label. If Jeff's labels are teacher-authored, this is already a broad form of task distillation. It supplies no alternative-option structure. Agreement measures imitation unless independently checked.
2. **Option-probability distillation:** for same decision and exact option mapping, `q_T = softmax(v/T)` from teacher logits and `p_T = softmax(s/T)` from student. A mixed loss can be `α CE(y,p_1)+(1−α) T² KL(q_T || p_T)`. Hinton uses the same temperature on both sides and T² compensation. α and T remain bounded experimental choices, not transplanted optimums. [S1 §2]
3. **Only probabilities available:** exact positive normalized probabilities determine logits up to an additive constant; temperature adjustment is proportional to `q^(1/T)`. Rounded/zeroed probabilities or top-k truncated API outputs may destroy the tail. With 2–6 choices, retain all candidate scores where possible. Raw MSE on uncentered logits unnecessarily matches an arbitrary offset; Hinton's high-T equivalence assumes per-case centered logits. [S1 §2.1]
4. **A decoder's true next-token probabilities** are its internal conditional token distribution. Restricting and renormalizing A–F yields probabilities conditional on the allowed-symbol event, not proof of probabilities of correctness. Record prompt, boundary tokenization, option mapping, temperature and permitted mass if available. Multi-token continuations require consistent joint scoring; do not take just their first token.
5. **Verbal confidence / generated probability JSON** is text predicted by the model, not the above logit distribution. It might be useful after separate reliability evidence, but cannot silently stand in for logit KD. Repeated sampled answers estimate a prompt/decoding-dependent answer frequency, also not an independent truth oracle.
6. **Independent truth:** programmatic rule checks/human verification and source labels have different provenance from same-teacher agreement. A smoother or more teacher-like student can still become less correct. Distillation can propagate teacher mistakes and source shortcuts. Do not qualify a teacher merely because it is larger or agrees with its own generated labels.

## Five decisive primary sources and their relevance gaps

### S1 — Hinton, Vinyals & Dean (2015), *Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network*
URL: https://arxiv.org/html/1503.02531

- **Original passage:** §1: “When the soft targets have high entropy, they provide much more information per training case than hard targets.” §2: “The same high temperature is used when training the distilled model”; gradients require multiplication by T² when mixing objectives.
- **Method/scale/data:** MNIST teacher has two 1,200-unit hidden layers; student two 800-unit layers, 60,000 training cases. §3 reports 146 errors for small ordinary net versus 74 distilled, teacher 67. Speech uses ~85M acoustic models, 2,000 hours/~700M frame cases and an ensemble of 10.
- **Counterevidence:** §2.1 says very negative logits may be noise, and a too-small student benefits from intermediate rather than extreme temperatures; §3's 30-unit student preferred T=2.5–4. Some illustrative missing-class bias corrections explicitly optimize test performance; they are NOT a held-out protocol to copy.
- **Jeff gap:** Fixed semantic classes and matched task distributions, not dynamic natural-language option sets or policy transfer. With only 2–6 options, binary tasks especially offer limited “dark” inter-class structure. It supports feasibility of KD, not a guaranteed gain or calibration claim.

### S2 — Hsieh et al. (2023), *Distilling Step-by-Step!*
URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2305.02301 ; official implementation https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/distilling-step-by-step/main/README.md

- **Original passage:** §3.2: “instead of using rationales as additional model inputs, we frame learning with rationales as a multi-task problem”; label and rationale generation have separate prefixes/losses. §4.4: single-task rationale+label generation “can at times leads to worse performance than standard finetuning, e.g., on ANLI and CQA.”
- **Method/scale/data:** 540B PaLM teacher, T5-Base 220M for data-efficiency study; T5 220M/770M/11B scale study; four benchmarks e-SNLI, ANLI, CommonsenseQA, SVAMP. §4.1/Fig.4 reports 220M T5 with 12.5% of e-SNLI outperforming label-only full-data training. This is a fraction of that source dataset, not 12.5% of Jeff. SVAMP has 800 training problems; adding 2,305 ASDiv examples was needed to close the reported unlabeled-setting gap using 11B T5 (§4.2). A 20B GPT-NeoX teacher gave smaller lifts (§4.4).
- **Implementation cross-check:** README explicitly separates GT vs LLM labels and task-prefix multitask vs standard training; recommends α=0.5. We did not run it or adopt its older CUDA dependencies.
- **Jeff gap:** Smallest main experimental T5 is ~10× Jeff, generative pretrained encoder–decoder rather than scalar encoder. Teacher is orders of magnitude beyond Qwen0.5B. Gains do **not require generating rationales at deployment**, but the demonstrated method **does require rationale-generation supervision during training**. No evidence here that a 22M encoder absorbs equivalent procedural behavior from scalar targets alone. Rationales can be post-hoc/wrong; paper itself cautions about complex reasoning/planning and inherited teacher bias.

### S3 — Robinson, Rytting & Wingate (2023), *Leveraging Large Language Models for Multiple Choice Question Answering*
URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2210.12353 ; official repository README https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BYU-PCCL/leveraging-llms-for-mcqa/main/README.md

- **Original passage:** §3: model predicts a single symbol and symbol probabilities “serve as a proxy for each answer's probability.” §4: “PPA measures order invariance irrespective of model ability to perform a task.” §5.1: cloze prompts perform best for GPT-3, whereas multiple-choice prompts always perform best for Instruct and Codex in the three-model-comparison tasks.
- **Method/scale/data:** Symbol-binding experiment: 100 sampled OpenBookQA questions, all 24 orders for four choices; models range 1.5B GPT-2 to 178B Jurassic-1. Broader comparison uses Codex/Davinci on 20 benchmarks; introduction reports mean 9.7 percentage-point advantage over cloze and wins on 16/20. Codex parameter count is not independently established in passages read; do not silently label it 175B.
- **Jeff gap:** Supports frozen answer-symbol scoring as a rational decoder baseline and checking order, not its superiority at 0.5B. Some GPT-2/CodeParrot/Instruct-Curie symbol binding is close to chance; even instruction tuning is not sufficient by itself. Joint options can enable direct comparison but also introduce position/symbol biases absent from independent pair encoding. Paper's “proxy” explicitly does not establish calibration/truth. One MCP pass may be longer than each CP pass; do not transplant its pass-count claim into a measured laptop speedup.

### S4 — Wei et al. (2021/2022), *Finetuned Language Models Are Zero-Shot Learners*
URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2109.01652

- **Original passage:** §4.2: “for the 8B and smaller models ... instruction tuning actually hurts performance on held-out tasks.” Model-capacity explanation is presented as a potential explanation, not demonstrated causality.
- **Method/scale/data:** FLAN instruction-tunes a 137B language model on 62 NLP datasets with held-out task clusters. Scale ablation: 422M, 2B, 8B, 68B, 137B on a ~40-task training split. §2.3 reports 30k steps/~60 hours on 128 TPUv3 cores for its principal run.
- **Jeff gap:** This is contrary evidence to assuming instruction-formatted multitask training automatically produces novel-task transfer. It is NOT proof that models below 8B cannot benefit: see S5. Different historical pretraining/mixture/objective make a universal capacity cutoff inappropriate, especially for already instruction-tuned Qwen.

### S5 — Longpre et al. (2023; current HTML title *Designing Data and Methods for Effective Instruction Tuning*, Flan Collection)
URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2301.13688

- **Original passage:** §3.3: “both Held-In and Held-Out tasks appear to benefit from adding hundreds of finetuning tasks”; held-in performance peaks around 200 tasks and later declines. They caution against assuming returns beyond 1,800 tasks without quality/diversity. §3.5: “the mixture weighting deserves as much attention to optimize results.”
- **Method/scale/data:** Most method ablations fix T5-XL 3B; scale study spans T5 Small/Base/Large/XL/XXL and subsets of 8 through 1,873 tasks (the HTML body also uses 1,836; avoid pretending a single exact count is consistently specified). Evaluation separates held-in QA/NLI, CoT, MMLU and BBH. §3.1 Table1 removing mixture balancing lowers MMLU zero-shot from 50.3 to 45.4, while benefits vary by metric. §3.3 includes positive smaller T5 results but T5-Small peaks before the maximum task count.
- **Jeff gap:** Updates S4's pessimistic small-model generalization with stronger data/methods, but is not a 22M-scale or 1,500-example result. Source balancing is a plausible control, not proof that equal-source weighting helps Jeff. Instruction pretraining is an accumulated asset; recreating thousands of tasks is not a bounded hackathon intervention. All main ablations use far larger models than Jeff.

## Minimal intervention/control menu for parent selection

These are proposal sketches only; no run, new label generation, or recipe change is authorized by this handoff.

**Low-dependency branch:** freeze checkpoint initialization, exact TRAIN release, token budget, update count and seed. Compare one selected train-only intervention (source weighting OR fixed mild smoothing, not both) to matched hard-CE control. Use the parent's existing reporting boundary: inherited60/new240, probe24 over12groups, and D3 separately. Report log loss plus accuracy/agreement and source/group units; smoothing that only improves confidence on correlated validation is not rule-transfer success. If source weighting, hold updates/decision exposure accounting explicit; more resampling is not free extra independent data.

**Conditional KD branch:** only if a teacher is independently useful and true TRAIN option scores are already available or separately authorized. Keep exact same hard labels, examples, model, updates; mixed hard+KD versus hard CE. Add entropy-matched uniform smoothing only if parent can afford that third arm, to distinguish teacher relational information from generic softening. For dynamic options map by option identity, not answer-symbol position. Do not use validation/D3 outputs as training material. Teacher score failures need explicit coverage, not silent exclusion. No claim that KD is primary before availability/quality gates pass.

**Tempting direction not warranted:** collect long CoT for every example, LoRA-tune Qwen to emit rationales, add reinforcement learning/self-consistency, and call it “sample-efficient reasoning.” This combines a new student, teacher, supervision type, output interface and token budget, at materially higher engineering/inference cost. S2 specifically warns that naïve rationale+label joint training can hurt. It would not isolate why Jeff failed M5 or distinguish more capacity/pretraining from a learning-strategy gain.
